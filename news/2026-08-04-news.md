# 🚨 Hotel Wi-Fi attacks use custom malware to breach Microsoft 365 accounts
**Source**: BleepingComputer
**Link**: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hotel-wi-fi-attacks-use-custom-malware-to-breach-microsoft-365-accounts/
**Date**: 2026-08-04

## 📰 Summary of the Threat
The recently discovered global campaign targeting hospitality Wi-Fi networks has been linked to the Russian threat actor Midnight Blizzard, also known as APT29. This campaign involves the use of custom malware designed to breach Microsoft 365 accounts. The attackers exploit vulnerabilities in hotel Wi-Fi networks to gain initial access, and then utilize the custom malware to steal credentials and gain unauthorized access to Microsoft 365 accounts. The technical mechanism of the vulnerability involves the exploitation of weaknesses in Wi-Fi network security, potentially including weak passwords, outdated firmware, or misconfigured access points.

The threat is particularly concerning as it targets individuals and organizations that use hotel Wi-Fi networks, which can include business travelers, remote workers, and other individuals who rely on public Wi-Fi for access to sensitive information. The use of custom malware indicates a high level of sophistication and intent on the part of the attackers, who are likely motivated by the potential for financial gain or espionage. The fact that the campaign has been linked to a known threat actor suggests that the attackers have a significant level of resources and expertise at their disposal.

The attack highlights the importance of robust Wi-Fi network security and the need for individuals and organizations to take steps to protect themselves when using public Wi-Fi networks. This includes using virtual private networks (VPNs), avoiding the use of public Wi-Fi for sensitive activities, and ensuring that devices and software are kept up to date with the latest security patches. Microsoft 365 accounts are a particularly attractive target due to the sensitive information they often contain, and the potential for attackers to use compromised accounts to gain access to other systems and data.

## 🛠️ Actionable Defense & Mitigation Checklist
1. **Implement Wi-Fi network segmentation**: Divide the Wi-Fi network into separate segments for guests and staff, and restrict access to sensitive areas of the network. This can be achieved through the use of virtual local area networks (VLANs) and access control lists (ACLs).
2. **Use WPA3 encryption and configure access points securely**: Ensure that all access points are configured to use WPA3 encryption, and that passwords are complex and regularly changed. Additionally, disable WPS and limit the number of concurrent connections to prevent brute-force attacks.
3. **Deploy a network access control (NAC) system**: Implement a NAC system to control and manage network access based on user identity, location, and device type. This can help to prevent unauthorized access to the network and detect potential security threats.
4. **Conduct regular security audits and penetration testing**: Regularly conduct security audits and penetration testing to identify vulnerabilities in the Wi-Fi network and other systems. This can help to identify potential entry points for attackers and ensure that security controls are effective.
5. **Implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) for Microsoft 365 accounts**: Require MFA for all Microsoft 365 accounts to prevent attackers from gaining access to accounts using stolen or compromised credentials.
