# 🚨 Steam forum ClickFix attacks infect gamers with XMRig cryptominers
**Source**: BleepingComputer
**Link**: https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/steam-forum-clickfix-attacks-infect-gamers-with-xmrig-cryptominers/
**Date**: 2026-07-26

## 📰 Summary of the Threat
The Steam forum ClickFix attacks are a type of social engineering campaign that targets gamers on the Steam platform. The attackers create fake forum posts that appear to offer fixes for game-related issues or computer problems, but actually contain malicious links or attachments that infect devices with XMRig cryptominers. XMRig is a well-known cryptocurrency mining malware that uses system resources to mine Monero (XMR) cryptocurrency, often causing significant performance degradation and increased power consumption. The attackers likely use the Steam forum as a vector to reach a large audience of potential victims, exploiting their trust in the platform and the perceived legitimacy of the fake fix posts.

The technical mechanism of the vulnerability involves the use of social engineering tactics to trick users into downloading and executing the malicious payload. The attackers may use various techniques, such as phishing, spear phishing, or pretexting, to create convincing fake posts that appear to come from trusted sources. Once a user clicks on the malicious link or downloads the attachment, the XMRig cryptominer is installed on their device, allowing the attackers to hijack system resources for cryptocurrency mining. The malware may also communicate with command and control (C2) servers to receive updates, transmit stolen cryptocurrency, or exfiltrate sensitive data.

The targeted audience appears to be gamers who frequent the Steam discussion forums, particularly those seeking help with game-related issues or technical problems. The attackers may use keywords and hashtags related to popular games or technical topics to increase the visibility of their fake posts and reach a wider audience. As the attacks are highly targeted and rely on social engineering, it is essential for users to be cautious when interacting with links or attachments from unknown sources, even if they appear to come from trusted platforms like Steam.

## 🛠️ Actionable Defense & Mitigation Checklist
1. **Implement URL filtering and reputation-based blocking**: Configure web filtering solutions to block known malicious URLs and domains associated with XMRig cryptominers. Utilize reputation-based blocking to prevent users from accessing suspicious or untrusted websites.
2. **Enable endpoint detection and response (EDR) tools**: Deploy EDR solutions to monitor endpoint activity and detect potential XMRig infections. Configure EDR tools to alert on suspicious behavior, such as unusual system resource usage or communication with C2 servers.
3. **Conduct regular system and network audits**: Perform regular audits to identify and remediate potential vulnerabilities, such as outdated software or misconfigured systems. Use tools like Nmap or OpenVAS to scan for open ports and services, and prioritize patching and remediation efforts accordingly.
4. **Enforce strict attachment and link handling policies**: Establish policies to restrict users from downloading or executing attachments from unknown sources. Implement link scanning and sandboxing solutions to analyze links and attachments for potential malware before allowing users to access them.
5. **Develop and distribute user awareness training**: Create training programs to educate users on the risks of social engineering attacks, such as ClickFix campaigns, and provide guidance on safe browsing habits, link handling, and attachment management.
